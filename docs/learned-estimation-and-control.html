Learned estimation and control on SCALAR's real hardware

Five simulation experiments answering three questions: where a neural network helps state estimation, whether absolute pointing is possible without GNSS, and how to learn the magnetorquer configuration and derive LQR gains that actually point.

2026-09-11 · scalar-controls, untracked · simulation only, nothing verified on hardware

SensorsLSM6DSO gyro, LIS2MDL magnetometer, 7× VEML6031 light sensors, RV-3028 RTC. No GNSS, no star tracker.
Actuators (assumed)S3 coils on S6's three channels: 0.058 A·m² X, 0.058 Y, 0.0087 Z. Unmeasured, see C-09.
ProcessorsRP2350 (M33, FPU, 520 KB) with firmware. STM32L031 (M0+, 8 KB RAM, 32 KB flash) named as controller, no firmware.
Software ceilingSensors read at 1 Hz. Orbit 550 km / 98.61°, 37 % eclipse.

Experiment 1A network belongs at the sun sensor, nowhere else

The MEKF averages noise optimally; its 5–9° floor is systematic: Earth albedo, per-diode gain, cover-glass roll-off, face tilt. A 1.5k-parameter MLP (6 currents, plus the estimator's predicted nadir direction and dayside factor, → 32 → 32 → unit vector) replaces the opposing-pair reconstruction and feeds the MEKF unchanged. 1440 multiply-adds per second.

Verdict. Worth doing, as a sensor model in front of the filter, not as a replacement estimator. Magnetometer iron and gyro propagation gain nothing from learning.

Experiment 3Learning the coil matrix: pulse length beats sample rate

Euler's equation is linear in the dipole matrix and the residual spacecraft dipole once inertia is known, so the A9 burst record (coil current, body field, body rate) identifies both by least squares. Inertia shape is checkable from any free tumble (recovered to three decimals); inertia scale is not observable from motion and must come from CAD or the bench.

Verdict. The 320 ms detumble pulse is not an identification pulse. Ninety 2-second pulses (15 minutes) recover X/Y to 2–3 % and the weak Z coil to ~10 %. Sample at 5 Hz or better so the averaging windows can be placed, and give the campaign its own pulse-duration parameter.

Experiment 4Gains that point: periodic LQR, after rate damping

Two findings about the existing lqr.py. First, a sign bug: in this repo's convention the attitude error vector's time derivative is minus the body rate, so both LQR designs had positive attitude feedback and the demo only ever damped rate. Second, even corrected, the time-invariant LQR with ⊥B projection ends 90–110° from target, and a low-gain averaging law is out-muscled by the residual dipole.

The design that works puts the field inside the Riccati recursion: dipole is the input, and the output is a gain schedule over orbit phase.

Bryson weights 10°, 0.2°/s, ρ 0.3. Residual dipole 2.3 mA·m² in every run. Rate damping is m = k ω×B, the gyro form of B-dot.

Verdict. Damp first, then the periodic schedule. This is the C-26 supervisor structure: DetumbleManager as mode 1, the pointing law as mode 2. The schedule needs 256 phase bins; 64 is not enough.

PlacementWhat runs where

  • RP2350: estimator and MLP front end. It has the FPU, the memory, and the sensors.
  • STM32L031: current control with a link-loss timeout. A 256-bin gain schedule is 18 KB float32 or 9 KB int16 against 32 KB of flash, so holding the gains on the RP2350 and sending dipole commands over the UART is the safer split. Either way the link is inside the loop (C-27).
  • Before any of it: measure the coil matrix. Every gain above is for a coil set no source confirms.
